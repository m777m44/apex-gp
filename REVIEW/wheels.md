2 findings (0 fatal).

## 1. [MAJOR] car/wheels.js

**Defect:** The wheel cover is a featureless painted disc and all twelve slots are dead black. At `wheel` the wheel is ~520 px tall and you cannot see a brake disc, a caliper, a duct or any glow through a single slot; the cover itself measures Ymean 51.0 with sd 17.2 across a 120x120 patch, i.e. one soft radial gradient and nothing else. There is no brake glow anywhere in any of the five captures.

**Prescribed fix:** Put the disc and caliper geometry behind the cover and light it — `setBrakeGlow` needs a real emissive ramp that is visible through the slots at `wheel` and `chase`. On the cover itself: radial anisotropy (the part is machined), a fastener ring at the outer edge, and a brake-dust / tyre-marble term in the ORM so it is not one clean value.

## 2. [MAJOR] textures/procedural.js `rubber()` + car/wheels.js

**Defect:** The tyre has no material response at all. `rubber()` writes a single roughness (0.86 minus a blotch term) across the whole tile with no dependence on the profile coordinate, so crown, shoulder and sidewall are the same surface. At `wheel` the result is a uniform grain field with zero specular sheen, no mould flash line, no graining, no scrub band — it reads as felt, and it is the largest object in most car frames.

**Prescribed fix:** Drive roughness from `tyreProfileMap`'s v: crown ~0.55 (a scrubbed slick is glossy), shoulder ~0.78, sidewall ~0.68 with a broad low-frequency sheen. Add a circumferential mould-flash ridge and radial vent pips to the height field, and let tyreTemp modulate crown gloss so a hot tyre shines.
